About

Anie Ajamian

Hi, I'm Anie, a product designer in Los Angeles. Most of what I've worked on is stuff people have to do rather than stuff they want to do. Refill a prescription. Pay for something. Book a room. Nobody chooses to do any of it, and it's usually more stressful than it needs to be, which is the part that bothers me. Not because these problems are genuinely that hard, but because making them simpler was never anyone's job.

I started in graphic and web design, doing brand and marketing sites, before I moved into product. What changed how I work were the years inside early and mid-stage startups, working closely with founders, PMs, and engineers. I stopped thinking about the one perfect screen and started paying attention to everything around it that decides whether the screen can even exist. At Wyndham, a redesign of the booking flow had tested well and then cost the business conversion. The org's response was to stop touching it. What tests well and what actually works are different questions, and knowing which one you're answering is most of the job.

A lot of that turned out to be about people rather than pixels. I talk to the actual users when I can, not just session recordings and A/B results. At Medly, patient conversations shaped what we built next more than any test did. I also care about how a team runs. The best teams I've been on ran real retros and asked each other for help constantly, and nobody read that as weakness. When a junior designer on my team was stuck and waiting to be told what to do, I asked her what she thought and why. She had an answer. She just hadn't been asked. That's most of what I think leadership is.

Outside of work, I'm usually traveling, spending time with family, or looking at other people's interiors. Travel is where a lot of my inspiration comes from, meeting people who live differently and seeing how other places handle the same everyday things. Product, hotel, or my own living room, it's always the small details that get me.

What I believe

Design isn't the last step

I've cared about art and design my whole life, and it has never really felt like work. The world is full of things that are more complicated than they need to be, and design is what quietly takes that weight off people. When it's done well, you don't notice it at all. That's the part I find worth doing.

Design still tends to get treated as a finishing layer, something you add once the real decisions are already made. I want to be in the room for those decisions instead of cleaning up after them. And I like bringing other people along, showing a founder or an engineer how much easier something can be to use, and watching them start to care about it too.
