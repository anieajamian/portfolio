Overview

Rooms & Rates is the highest-stakes screen in Wyndham's booking flow: the moment travelers compare room types and pricing before they commit. As part of a 1.5-year effort to replatform Wyndham's entire website and mobile apps, I led the redesign of this experience across Wyndham's franchise ecosystem, weighing usability gains against conversion risk at the most sensitive step of the funnel.

The Problem

Users struggled to compare rooms because of inconsistent pricing structures, dense content, and unclear hierarchy. Because Wyndham operates as a franchise system, room details and pricing vary widely from property to property, since franchisees own their own content. Previous redesigns by other designers had improved usability on paper but hurt conversion, so the organization had grown cautious about touching this flow at all. My own first redesign, A/B tested before the replatform and never implemented, ran into the same problem:

<1%
difference in overall bookings
−5%
drop in mobile bookings

A bigger visual change didn’t move bookings, and it cost us on mobile. That result shaped every call that followed.

Objectives:

  • Improve comparison: help users evaluate room types and rates side by side
  • Clarify pricing: build real hierarchy and differentiation between rate types
  • Reduce cognitive load at a high-intent decision point
  • Design for scale across a franchise system with variable content
Issues surfaced from past usability testing.

Constraints

Franchisees own room & rate content, so descriptions and amenities are inconsistent property to property
Some rooms surfaced 5+ rate options, well past the 3–4 users could comfortably compare
No standardized naming convention for rooms or rates (e.g. redundant "non-smoking" labels on every room in an already non-smoking hotel)
Rate types lacked clear differentiation, making perks and inclusions hard to parse

Approach

I benchmarked how major hotel and travel platforms handle room and rate selection. Two dominant patterns emerged:

Pattern A

Single-page comparison

Marriott, Hilton, Hyatt, IHG

  • • Everything compared on one screen
Pattern B

Separated room / rate flow

Choice, plus OTAs like Expedia and Booking.com

  • • Extra navigation for deeper pricing detail
Direction

Given the booking flow's sensitivity to conversion, I focused the near-term solution on improving clarity within the existing single-step model, while exploring modal-based rate expansion as a longer-term direction.

Design directions:

  • Limit the number of visible rate options per room
  • Improve scanability with larger imagery and simpler hierarchy
  • Clarify rate differentiation and the visibility of key interactions
  • Explore modal and layout variations to support scalability without adding steps

Validation

After stakeholder alignment, we moved forward with an incremental approach, refining the existing list-based layout while deferring more experimental patterns for post-launch validation, given the conversion sensitivity of the flow. Before launch, I worked with our UX researcher on a usability gut check (UserTesting), comparing the new design against the live site. The goal wasn’t to optimize. It was to confirm nothing in the new design stood out as wrong for users.

Nothing raised a red flag. Most of the friction users raised was what we’d already seen on the original design:

What worked
  • ✓ Visual clarity and stronger imagery
  • ✓ Easier navigation
  • ✓ No new usability problems introduced
Known issues, still present
  • ○ About a third still had to open room details to compare, and inconsistent room naming made it harder
  • ○ Rate titles were vague and confusing, so it was hard to tell rates apart or see what each included
  • ○ About a third missed or misunderstood the Price / Points toggle

Because these issues weren’t new, we didn’t change the design in response. We kept it close to the current site and planned to test these pieces one at a time after launch.

Outcome

The safest move wasn't the most ambitious redesign. It was staging change deliberately in a flow where past ambition had already cost conversion.

Given the scale and complexity of a franchise-driven ecosystem, we kept the launch design close to the current site and turned what we heard in testing into a plan for incremental tests after launch:

Test after launch
  • • Filters and Price / Points toggle placement
  • • Concise, clearer rate descriptions
  • • Room attributes in the list view (occupancy, square footage, amenities)
  • • Richer rate context like cancellation policy
Longer-term
  • • Standardized room & rate naming across properties
  • • Side-by-side comparison tool

Rooms & Rates is one piece of a 1.5-year replatform of Wyndham’s entire website and mobile apps, so results are still pending. Instead of A/B testing a full redesign again, like the one that cost mobile bookings, we kept the launch design close to the current site and will test individual changes after launch. Success means holding baseline booking performance while improving conversion, decision confidence, and rate-selection efficiency.
